1439:  T_{j_1, \dots, j_m}(W; x)
1440:  $$
1441:  be the remainder of the division of $\Delta_{j_1, \dots, j_m}W(x, \dots, x)$ by $f(x)$.  Since $f$ is a monic polynomial, $T_{j_1, \dots, j_m}(W; x)$ will have integer coefficients, and its degree is less than or equal to $n-1$.
1442: 
1443: Our first task is to find an upper bound for the sizes of the coefficients of the polynomials $T_{j_1, \dots, j_m}(W; x)$. For $W \in \mathcal W$ we have
1444: $$
1445: abc(\Delta_{j_1, \dots, j_m} W) \leq 2^{r_1 +\dots + r_m} B_1 \leq 2^{mr_1} B_1 < B_1^{1+\delta}
1446: $$
1447: by \eqref{roth:three-small-factors}. Indeed, \eqref{roth:eq32} gives
1448: $mr_1\log2<\tfrac12\delta^2r_1\log q_1<\delta\log B_1$.
1449: When we replace $x_1, \dots, x_m$ by the same variable $x$ the absolute value of the coefficients of the resulting polynomial is at most the number of terms of the polynomial, which is at most $r$, multiplied by the coefficient bound $B_1^{1+\delta}$. Now,
1450: $$
1451: r = (r_1 + 1) \cdots (r_m + 1) \leq 2^{r_1 + \dots + r_m} \leq 2^{mr_1} < B_1^\delta,
1452: $$
1453: and this implies that $rB_1^{1+ \delta} < B_1^{1 + 2 \delta}$.  Consequently, the coefficients of $\Delta_{j_1, \dots, j_m}W (x, \dots, x)$ are bounded in absolute value by $B_1^{1+ 2\delta}$. Next we examine the effect of division by $f$ on these coefficients. Write
1454: $$
1455: \Delta_{j_1, \dots, j_m}W (x, \dots, x) = w_s x^s + w_{s-1} x^{s-1} + \cdots + w_0.
1456: $$
1457: When we divide this polynomial by $f$, the first operation we do (assuming $s \geq n$) is to subtract $w_s x^{s-n} f(x)$ and this gives a new polynomial whose coefficients are either of the form $w_\nu-a_{s-\nu}w_s$ (when $1\leq s-\nu\leq n$) or of the form $w_\nu$.  Since the $a_{s-\nu}$'s
1458: are bounded by $A$, the absolute values of the resulting coefficients are at most $$(1 + A)  \cdot abc\, (\Delta_{j_1, \dots, j_m}W (x, \dots, x)) \leq (1+A) B_1^{1 + 2 \delta}.$$
1459: If the input polynomial is zero, its remainder is zero. Otherwise the
1460: number of leading-term eliminations is at most $\max(0,s-n+1)$: there are
1461: none when $s<n$, and each elimination lowers the degree by at least one.
1462: It follows that
1463: \[
1464: abc(T_{j_1,\dots,j_m}(W;x))
1465:  \leq(1+A)^{\max(0,s-n+1)}B_1^{1+2\delta}
1466:  \leq(1+A)^{mr_1}B_1^{1+2\delta}<B_1^{1+3\delta},
1467: \]
