121: We start by defining differential operators in $p\geq1$ variables.
122: For a multi-index $i=(i_1,\dots,i_p)\in\N^p$, define the \emph{Hasse differential operator}
123: $\Delta_i$ by its effect on monomials:
124: \[
125: \Delta_i(x_1^{k_1}\cdots x_p^{k_p})=
126: \begin{cases}
127: \displaystyle\left(\prod_{j=1}^p {k_j\choose i_j}\right)
128:  x_1^{k_1-i_1}\cdots x_p^{k_p-i_p},& i_j\leq k_j\text{ for every }j,\\[3pt]
129: 0,&\text{otherwise}.
130: \end{cases}
131: \]
132: We extend this operator linearly to all polynomials. It has order
133: $|i|=i_1+\cdots+i_p$. The definition works over $\Z$, $\Q$, or $\R$
134: (and over any commutative semiring by casting the binomial coefficients).
135: Over a $\Q$-algebra it agrees with
136: \[
137: \Delta_i=\frac{1}{i_1!\cdots i_p!}
138:  \left(\frac{\partial}{\partial x_1}\right)^{i_1}\cdots
139:  \left(\frac{\partial}{\partial x_p}\right)^{i_p}.
140: \]
141: When $i_j\leq k_j$, one may equivalently write
142: ${k_j\choose i_j}={k_j\choose k_j-i_j}$.
143: Unless explicitly stated otherwise, a differential operator below means one
144: of these individual normalised operators, not an arbitrary linear combination.
145: This algebraic definition avoids the technicalities of Fr\'echet derivatives.

147: \lean{Represent polynomials by \texttt{MvPolynomial} and multi-indices
148: by finitely supported maps $\sigma\to_0\N$. Define each $\Delta_d$ as a
149: linear map and prove the coefficient identity
150: $[X^e]\Delta_dP=(\prod_j\binom{e_j+d_j}{d_j})[X^{e+d}]P$,
151: where $[X^e]$ denotes coefficient extraction; this shows that distinct
152: surviving monomials do not merge under differentiation. Use the univariate
571: \begin{lem}\label{lem:index-of-derivative}
572:     Suppose $k_1, \dots, k_p$ are non-negative integers such that $\Delta_{k_1, \dots, k_p} P$ is not identically zero. Then
573:     $$
574:     \ind(\Delta_{k_1, \dots, k_p} P; \alpha_1, \dots, \alpha_p; r_1, \dots, r_p) \geq \ind( P; \alpha_1, \dots, \alpha_p; r_1, \dots, r_p) - \left(\frac{k_1}{r_1}  + \cdots + \frac{k_p}{r_p}\right).
575:     $$
576: \end{lem}
577: \begin{proof}
578:     Choose a multi-index $i$ attaining the index of $\Delta_kP$.
579:     The Hasse composition identity gives
580:     \[
581:     \Delta_i\Delta_kP=
582:        \left(\prod_{j=1}^p{i_j+k_j\choose i_j}\right)\Delta_{i+k}P.
583:     \]
584:     Since its value at $\alpha$ is nonzero, $(\Delta_{i+k}P)(\alpha)\ne0$.
585:     Therefore
586:     $\ind(P)\leq\sum_j(i_j+k_j)/r_j
587:        =\ind(\Delta_kP)+\sum_jk_j/r_j$, as required.
588: \end{proof}
