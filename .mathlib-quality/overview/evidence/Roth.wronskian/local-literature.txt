157: \section{Wronskians}
158: 
159: If $l\geq1$ and $\phi_0(t), \dots, \phi_{l-1}(t)$ are $l$ polynomials in the variable $t$, we call the determinant
160: $$
161: W(\phi_0, \dots, \phi_{l-1})(t) = \det \left(\frac{1}{\mu !} \frac{d^\mu}{d t^\mu} \phi_\nu(t)\right)_{0 \leq \mu \leq l-1 \atop 0 \leq \nu \leq l-1}
162: $$
163: the Wronskian of the polynomials.
164: 
165: \begin{lem}
166:     If $\phi_0(t), \dots, \phi_{l-1}(t)$ have rational coefficients, then the Wronskian vanishes if and only if they are linearly dependent, i.e., there are rational constants $c_0, \dots, c_{l-1}$, not all of which are zero, such that
167:     $$
168:     c_0 \phi_0(t) + \dots + c_{l-1} \phi_{l-1}(t) = 0.
169:     $$
170: \end{lem}
171: \begin{proof}
172:     Linear dependence gives a nontrivial constant linear relation between
173:     the columns, so the determinant vanishes.
174:     Conversely, suppose the polynomials are linearly independent. By invertible
175:     column operations over $\Q$, replace them by a basis $g_0,\dots,g_{l-1}$ of
176:     their span having distinct degrees $d_0<\cdots<d_{l-1}$.
177:     Such a basis is obtained by eliminating equal leading degrees: the
178:     successive quotients of the degree filtration have dimension at most one.
179:     If $A\in\mathrm{GL}_l(\Q)$ is this change-of-basis matrix, then
180:     $W(g_0,\dots,g_{l-1})=W(\phi_0,\dots,\phi_{l-1})\det A$.
