969:     Next, suppose $p \geq 2$ is an integer and that the lemma is true for $m= p-1$. We apply Lemma \ref{roth:lem6}. In order to do this we will need to estimate $M$ and $\Phi$ of Lemma \ref{roth:lem6}.
970: 
971:     We have
972:     $$
973:     M = (r_1 + 1)^{pl} l! 2^{plr_1} q_1^{\delta l r_1} \leq \left((r_1 + 1)^{p} l 2^{pr_1} q_1^{\delta r_1}\right)^l.
974:     $$
975:     Next, $l \leq r_p+1 < r_1 + 1 \leq 2^{r_1}$. Consequently,
976:     $$
977:     M < \left(2^{(2p+1)r_1}q_1^{\delta r_1} \right)^l < \left(e^{(2p+1)r_1}q_1^{\delta r_1} \right)^l.
978:     $$
979:     From Equation \ref{roth:eq21} for $m = p$, we have $2p+1 < \delta p^{-1} \log q_1$, and this gives
980:     $$
981:     M < \left(e^{(\delta p^{-1} \log q_1)r_1}q_1^{\delta r_1} \right)^l= \left(q_1^{(\delta p^{-1} )r_1}q_1^{\delta r_1} \right)^l= q_1^{\delta_1 l r_1}
982:     $$
983:     with $\delta_1 = \delta(1+p^{-1})$. Hence,
984:     $$
985:     \Theta_1(M; q_p; lr_p) \leq \Theta_1(q_1^{\delta_1 l r_1}; q_p; l r_p) \leq \frac{\log (q_1^{\delta_1 l r_1})}{l r_p \log q_p} \leq \frac{\delta_1 l r_1 \log q_1}{l r_1 \log q_1} = \delta_1,
986:     $$
987:     by Lemma \ref{roth:lem5}. Next,
988:     $$
989:     \Theta_{p-1}(M; q_1, \dots, q_{p-1}; lr_1, \dots, lr_{p-1}) \leq \Theta_{p-1}(q_1^{\delta_1 l r_1}; q_1, \dots, q_{p-1}; lr_1, \dots, lr_{p-1}).
990:     $$
991:     We verify the induction hypotheses for dimension $p-1$, parameter
992:     $\delta_1=\delta(1+1/p)$, and degrees $lr_1,\dots,lr_{p-1}$.
993:     First,
994:     \[
995:     0<(p-1)\delta_1<\frac{(p-1)(p+1)}{p^2}<1.
996:     \]
997:     Since $\delta_1>\delta$ and $l\geq1$, the degree-ratio and final-degree
998:     bounds follow from \eqref{roth:eq20}. Moreover,
999:     \[
1000:     \log q_1>\delta^{-1}p(2p+1)
1001:        >\delta_1^{-1}(p-1)(2p-1).
1002:     \]
1003:     The balanced-logarithm inequalities are unchanged when every degree is
1004:     multiplied by $l$. Hence all four hypotheses hold. By the induction hypothesis,
1005:     $$
1006:     \Theta_{p-1}(q_1^{\delta_1 l r_1}; q_1, \dots, q_{p-1}; lr_1, \dots, lr_{p-1}) < 10^{p-1} \delta_1^{(1/2)^{p-1}}.
1007:     $$
1008:     Then
1009:     $$
1010:     \Phi < \delta_1 +  10^{p-1} \delta_1^{(1/2)^{p-1}} = \delta (1+p^{-1}) + 10^{p-1} \delta^{(1/2)^{p-1}} (1+p^{-1})^{(1/2)^{p-1}}
1011:     $$
1012:     $$
1013:     < 2 \delta + 2 \cdot 10^{p-1} \delta^{(1/2)^{p-1}} < 3\cdot 10^{p-1} \delta^{(1/2)^{p-1}}.
1014:     $$
1015:     Here we have used the fact that $\delta_1 < 2 \delta$ and
1016:     $$
1017:     (1+p^{-1})^{(1/2)^{p-1}} < 2,
1018:     $$
1019:     which are both easily verified. Also $0<\delta<1$, so
1020:     $\delta\leq\delta^{2^{-(p-1)}}$, justifying the last absorption above.
1021:     For every allowed $l$, Lemma \ref{roth:lem6} therefore gives the same
1022:     strict upper bound, and the maximum is over a nonempty finite set.
1023:     Set $a=\delta^{2^{-p}}$. Then
1024:     $\delta^{2^{-(p-1)}}=a^2\leq a$ and $\delta^{1/2}\leq a$. Consequently,
1025:     \begin{align*}
1026:     \Theta_p(q_1^{\delta r_1};q_1,\dots,q_p;r_1,\dots,r_p)
1027:     &<2\left(3\cdot10^{p-1}a^2+
1028:        \sqrt3\,10^{(p-1)/2}a+\delta^{1/2}\right)\\
1029:     &\leq2\left(\frac3{10}+\frac{\sqrt3}{10^{(p+1)/2}}
1030:                          +\frac1{10^p}\right)10^pa\\
1031:     &\leq2\left(\frac3{10}+\frac{\sqrt3}{10^{3/2}}
1032:                          +\frac1{10^2}\right)10^pa
1033:       <10^pa.
1034:     \end{align*}
