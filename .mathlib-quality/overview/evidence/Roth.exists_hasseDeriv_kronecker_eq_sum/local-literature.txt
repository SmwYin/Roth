182:     Write $a_\nu\ne0$ for the leading coefficient of $g_\nu$ and set
183:     $D=\sum_\nu d_\nu-l(l-1)/2\geq0$. The coefficient of $t^D$ in the new
184:     Wronskian is
185:     \[
186:     \left(\prod_{\nu=0}^{l-1}a_\nu\right)
187:     \det\left({d_\nu\choose\mu}\right)_{\mu,\nu=0}^{l-1}
188:     =\left(\prod_{\nu=0}^{l-1}a_\nu\right)
189:       \frac{\prod_{0\leq i<j<l}(d_j-d_i)}{\prod_{\mu=0}^{l-1}\mu!}\ne0.
190:     \]
191:     Indeed, $\binom{X}{\mu}$ has degree $\mu$ and leading coefficient
192:     $1/\mu!$, so its evaluation determinant is the Vandermonde determinant
193:     multiplied by $\prod_\mu1/\mu!$. All other contributions have lower total
194:     degree. Thus the new Wronskian, and hence the original one, is nonzero.
195: \end{proof}

206: Suppose $l\geq1$ and $\phi_0(x_1, \dots, x_p), \phi_1(x_1, \dots, x_p), \dots, \phi_{l-1}(x_1, \dots, x_p)$ are $l$ polynomials in the $p$ variables $x_1, \dots, x_p$. 209: $$

212: \begin{lem}
213:     If $\phi_0, \dots, \phi_{l-1}$ are polynomials over a characteristic-zero field and are linearly dependent over that field, then all of their generalized Wronskians are identically zero.
214: \end{lem}
215: \begin{proof}
216:     Let $\sum_\nu c_\nu\phi_\nu=0$ be a nontrivial dependence relation
217:     over the coefficient field. Applying each $\Delta_\mu$ gives
218:     $\sum_\nu c_\nu\Delta_\mu\phi_\nu=0$. Choose $\nu_0$ with
219:     $c_{\nu_0}\ne0$ and express that column as a linear combination of the
220:     other columns. Multilinearity and alternation of the determinant then
221:     give zero.
222: \end{proof}

224: \begin{lem}\label{roth:lem1}
225:     If $\phi_0(x_1, \dots, x_p), \dots, \phi_{l-1}(x_1, \dots, x_p)$ are $l$ linearly independent polynomials in $p$ variables with rational coefficients, then at least one of their generalized Wronskians is not identically zero.
226: \end{lem}
227: \begin{proof}
228:     Let $k\geq2$ be an integer which is bigger than the degrees of the polynomials
229:     $\phi_0, \phi_1, \dots, \phi_{l-1}$ in each of the variables $x_1, \dots, x_p$. We will fix $k$ throughout this argument so we will often suppress the dependence of our objects on $k$. For each $\nu$, $0 \leq \nu \leq l-1$, let
230:     $$
231:     h_\nu(t) = \phi_\nu(t, t^k, t^{k^2}, \dots, t^{k^{p-1}})
232:     $$
233:     as polynomials of the variable $t$. We claim that these polynomials are linearly independent. For each $\nu$ write
234:     $$
235:     \phi_\nu(x_1, \dots, x_p) = \sum_{s_1=0}^{k-1} \dots \sum_{s_p = 0}^{k-1} b^{(\nu)}(s_1, \dots, s_p) x_1^{s_1} \cdots x_p^{s_p}.
236:     $$
237:     Suppose we have a linear dependence relation of the form
238:     $$
239:     \sum_{\nu=0}^{l-1} c_\nu h_\nu(t) = 0.
240:     $$
241:     We have
242:     $$
243:     \sum_{\nu=0}^{l-1} c_\nu \sum_{s_1=0}^{k-1} \dots \sum_{s_p = 0}^{k-1} b^{(\nu)}(s_1, \dots, s_p) t^{s_1 + k s_2 + \dots + k^{p-1} s_p} = 0.
244:     $$
245: 
246: FACT: Since each $s_i$ is strictly smaller than $k$, the representation of any natural number in the form
247: $$
248: s_1 + k s_2 + \dots + k^{p-1} s_p
249: $$
250: is unique.
251: 
252: Comparing the coefficient of $t^{s_1+ks_2+\cdots+k^{p-1}s_p}$ gives
253: \[
254: \sum_{\nu=0}^{l-1}c_\nu b^{(\nu)}(s_1,\dots,s_p)=0
255: \qquad(0\leq s_j<k).
256: \]
257: It follows, by collecting coefficients of the multivariable monomials, that
258: \[
259: \sum_{\nu=0}^{l-1}c_\nu\phi_\nu
260:  =\sum_{0\leq s_1,\dots,s_p<k}
261:    \left(\sum_{\nu=0}^{l-1}c_\nu b^{(\nu)}(s_1,\dots,s_p)\right)
262:    x_1^{s_1}\cdots x_p^{s_p}=0.
263: \]
264: The linear independence of the $\phi_\nu$ implies that every $c_\nu$ is zero.
265: 
266: \lean{Define Kronecker substitution as the algebra homomorphism
267: $x_j\mapsto t^{k^{j-1}}$, and prove injectivity only on the subspace where
268: every individual exponent is less than $k$. This restriction is essential:
269: when $p\geq2$, the nonzero polynomial $x_2-x_1^k$ maps to zero. Uniqueness
270: of base-$k$ expansions gives the restricted coefficient comparison, which
271: then transports the required linear independence.}

273: We conclude that the Wronskian
274: $$
275: W(h_0, \dots, h_{l-1})
276: $$
277: is not identically zero. We now relate this Wronskian to the Wronskians of the multivariable polynomials $\phi_\nu$.

279: Set
280: $$
281: \gamma(t) = (t, t^k, t^{k^2}, \dots, t^{k^{p-1}}).
282: $$
283: For a polynomial $f$ in $x_1, \dots, x_p$, the composite $f(\gamma(t))$ is a polynomial in the single variable $t$. We now relate its derivatives to the partial derivatives of $f$.
284: 
285: FACT: For each $\nu$,
286: $$
287: \frac{d}{dt}h_\nu(t) = \frac{\partial \phi_\nu} {\partial x_1 } (\gamma(t)) + k t^{k-1} \frac{\partial \phi_\nu} {\partial x_2 } (\gamma(t)) + \dots + k^{p-1}t^{k^{p-1}-1}\frac{\partial \phi_\nu} {\partial x_p } (\gamma(t)).
288: $$
289: 
290: This is the algebraic chain rule for polynomial substitution. The higher derivatives are handled by the uniform expansion below.
291: 
292: Then for each $\mu$, there is a natural number $r(\mu)$ depending only on $\mu$, polynomials $f^\mu_1(t), \dots, f^\mu_{r(\mu)}(t)$ with rational coefficients, and differential operators $\Delta_\mu^{(1)}, \dots, \Delta_\mu^{({r(\mu)})}$ of order less than or equal to $\mu$ such that for all $\nu$
293: $$
294: \frac{d^\mu}{dt^\mu} h_\nu(t) = f^\mu_1(t) (\Delta_\mu^{(1)}\phi_\nu)(\gamma(t)) + \dots + f^\mu_{r(\mu)}(t) (\Delta_\mu^{({r(\mu)})}\phi_\nu)(\gamma(t)).
295: $$
296: 
297: For completeness, this follows by induction on $\mu$, uniformly for all
298: input polynomials. For $\mu=0$, use the identity operator with coefficient $1$.
299: If a term at stage $\mu$ is $a(t)(\Delta_d\phi)(\gamma(t))$, its derivative is
300: \[
301: a'(t)(\Delta_d\phi)(\gamma(t))+
302:  a(t)\sum_{j=1}^p\gamma'_j(t)(d_j+1)
303:                  (\Delta_{d+e_j}\phi)(\gamma(t)).
304: \]
305: Here $\partial_j\Delta_d=(d_j+1)\Delta_{d+e_j}$ follows directly on
306: monomials. The orders are at most $\mu+1$, and all coefficients are rational
307: polynomials independent of $\phi$. Thus the same finite list works for every
308: $\phi_\nu$; $p$ and $k$ remain fixed throughout.
309: \lean{Treat $\phi\mapsto\phi(\gamma(t))$ as an algebra homomorphism and
310: prove the first-order chain rule using
311: algebraic partial derivatives\footnote{\url{https://leanprover-community.github.io/mathlib4_docs/Mathlib/Algebra/MvPolynomial/PDeriv.html}}
312: and polynomial induction, rather than analytic differentiation.
313: Represent the higher-order expansion by a finitely supported family of
314: coefficient polynomials indexed by $d$ with $|d|\leq\mu$; the recurrence

316: iterated derivatives to Hasse derivatives by factorial scaling only over
317: $\Q$.}
