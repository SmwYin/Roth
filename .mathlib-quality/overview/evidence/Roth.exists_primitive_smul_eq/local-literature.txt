414:     GW = \det \left(\sum_{\rho =0}^{l-1} \Delta_\mu \frac{1}{\nu !} \frac{\partial^\nu}{\partial x_p^\nu} \phi_\rho (x_p) \psi_\rho(x_1, \dots, x_{p-1})\right)_{0 \leq \mu \leq l-1 \atop 0 \leq \nu \leq l-1}
415:     $$
416:     $$
417:     = \det \left( \Delta_\mu \frac{1}{\nu !} \frac{\partial^\nu}{\partial x_p^\nu} R \right)_{0 \leq \mu \leq l-1 \atop 0 \leq \nu \leq l-1}.
418:     $$
419: 
420:     If we let $F = GW$, then $F$ is expressible in the form required by the lemma. Since $R$ has integral coefficients, so does $F$. Choose $g\in\Q^\times$ so that $U=gG$ is an integral primitive
421: polynomial, by clearing denominators and then dividing by the gcd of its
422: coefficients. Put $V=g^{-1}W$. For a coefficient $v_b$ of $V$ and any
423: coefficient $u_a$ of $U$, the product $u_av_b$ is a coefficient of $F$:
424: the variables of the two factors are disjoint. It is therefore an integer.
425: Primitivity gives a finite integral linear combination $\sum_a z_au_a=1$,
426: so $v_b=\sum_a z_a(u_av_b)$ is an integer. Thus $U,V$ are integral and
427: nonzero, as required.
428: \lean{Construct the separated decomposition over $\Q$ from a basis of the
429: span of the coefficient polynomials of $R$ in $x_p$. Isolate the integral
430: rescaling as a separate lemma: if integer coefficients $u_a$ have gcd $1$
431: and every product $u_av_b$ is integral, then each rational $v_b$ is integral
432: by a B\'ezout relation. Keep $U\ne0$ and $V\ne0$ explicit, since the degree
433: comparisons need both facts.}
434: 
435:     Finally, since the variables are disjoint and both factors are nonzero, the individual degrees of $U,V$ equal the corresponding degrees of $F$. Since $F$ is a determinant of an $l\times l$ matrix whose entries are polynomials of degree at most $r_j$ in $x_j$, $1 \leq j \leq p$, $F$ will be of degree at most $l r_j$ in each variable $x_j$.
