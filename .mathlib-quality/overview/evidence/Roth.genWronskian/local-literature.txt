206: Suppose $l\geq1$ and $\phi_0(x_1, \dots, x_p), \phi_1(x_1, \dots, x_p), \dots, \phi_{l-1}(x_1, \dots, x_p)$ are $l$ polynomials in the $p$ variables $x_1, \dots, x_p$. Also suppose $\Delta_0, \Delta_1, \dots, \Delta_{l-1}$ are $l$ differential operators such that for each $i$, $0 \leq i \leq l-1$, the order of $\Delta_i$ is at most $i$. We call the determinant
207: $$
208: W(\phi_0, \dots, \phi_{l-1}; \Delta_0, \dots, \Delta_{l-1}) = \det \left(\Delta_\mu \phi_\nu (x_1, \dots, x_p) \right)_{ 0 \leq \mu \leq l-1 \atop 0 \leq \nu \leq l-1}
209: $$
210: a generalized Wronskian. This is understood as a polynomial in the variables $x_1, \dots, x_p$.  If $p > 1$ and $l >1$ we have more than one generalized Wronskian.
211: 
212: \begin{lem}
213:     If $\phi_0, \dots, \phi_{l-1}$ are polynomials over a characteristic-zero field and are linearly dependent over that field, then all of their generalized Wronskians are identically zero.
214: \end{lem}
215: \begin{proof}
216:     Let $\sum_\nu c_\nu\phi_\nu=0$ be a nontrivial dependence relation
217:     over the coefficient field. Applying each $\Delta_\mu$ gives
218:     $\sum_\nu c_\nu\Delta_\mu\phi_\nu=0$. Choose $\nu_0$ with
219:     $c_{\nu_0}\ne0$ and express that column as a linear combination of the
220:     other columns. Multilinearity and alternation of the determinant then
221:     give zero.
222: \end{proof}

224: \begin{lem}\label{roth:lem1}
225:     If $\phi_0(x_1, \dots, x_p), \dots, \phi_{l-1}(x_1, \dots, x_p)$ are $l$ linearly independent polynomials in $p$ variables with rational coefficients, then at least one of their generalized Wronskians is not identically zero.
