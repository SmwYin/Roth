119: \section{Derivatives}
120: 
121: We start by defining differential operators in $p\geq1$ variables.
122: For a multi-index $i=(i_1,\dots,i_p)\in\N^p$, define the \emph{Hasse differential operator}
123: $\Delta_i$ by its effect on monomials:
124: \[
125: \Delta_i(x_1^{k_1}\cdots x_p^{k_p})=
126: \begin{cases}
127: \displaystyle\left(\prod_{j=1}^p {k_j\choose i_j}\right)
128:  x_1^{k_1-i_1}\cdots x_p^{k_p-i_p},& i_j\leq k_j\text{ for every }j,\\[3pt]
129: 0,&\text{otherwise}.
130: \end{cases}
131: \]
132: We extend this operator linearly to all polynomials. It has order
133: $|i|=i_1+\cdots+i_p$. The definition works over $\Z$, $\Q$, or $\R$
134: (and over any commutative semiring by casting the binomial coefficients).
135: Over a $\Q$-algebra it agrees with
136: \[
137: \Delta_i=\frac{1}{i_1!\cdots i_p!}
138:  \left(\frac{\partial}{\partial x_1}\right)^{i_1}\cdots
139:  \left(\frac{\partial}{\partial x_p}\right)^{i_p}.
140: \]
141: When $i_j\leq k_j$, one may equivalently write
142: ${k_j\choose i_j}={k_j\choose k_j-i_j}$.
143: Unless explicitly stated otherwise, a differential operator below means one
144: of these individual normalised operators, not an arbitrary linear combination.
145: This algebraic definition avoids the technicalities of Fr\'echet derivatives.

157: \section{Wronskians}
158: 
159: If $l\geq1$ and $\phi_0(t), \dots, \phi_{l-1}(t)$ are $l$ polynomials in the variable $t$, we call the determinant
160: $$
161: W(\phi_0, \dots, \phi_{l-1})(t) = \det \left(\frac{1}{\mu !} \frac{d^\mu}{d t^\mu} \phi_\nu(t)\right)_{0 \leq \mu \leq l-1 \atop 0 \leq \nu \leq l-1}
162: $$
163: the Wronskian of the polynomials.
164: 
165: \begin{lem}
166:     If $\phi_0(t), \dots, \phi_{l-1}(t)$ have rational coefficients, then the Wronskian vanishes if and only if they are linearly dependent, i.e., there are rational constants $c_0, \dots, c_{l-1}$, not all of which are zero, such that
167:     $$
168:     c_0 \phi_0(t) + \dots + c_{l-1} \phi_{l-1}(t) = 0.
169:     $$
170: \end{lem}
171: \begin{proof}
172:     Linear dependence gives a nontrivial constant linear relation between
173:     the columns, so the determinant vanishes.
174:     Conversely, suppose the polynomials are linearly independent. By invertible
175:     column operations over $\Q$, replace them by a basis $g_0,\dots,g_{l-1}$ of
176:     their span having distinct degrees $d_0<\cdots<d_{l-1}$.
177:     Such a basis is obtained by eliminating equal leading degrees: the
178:     successive quotients of the degree filtration have dimension at most one.
179:     If $A\in\mathrm{GL}_l(\Q)$ is this change-of-basis matrix, then
180:     $W(g_0,\dots,g_{l-1})=W(\phi_0,\dots,\phi_{l-1})\det A$.
181: 
182:     Write $a_\nu\ne0$ for the leading coefficient of $g_\nu$ and set
183:     $D=\sum_\nu d_\nu-l(l-1)/2\geq0$. The coefficient of $t^D$ in the new
184:     Wronskian is
185:     \[
186:     \left(\prod_{\nu=0}^{l-1}a_\nu\right)
187:     \det\left({d_\nu\choose\mu}\right)_{\mu,\nu=0}^{l-1}
188:     =\left(\prod_{\nu=0}^{l-1}a_\nu\right)
189:       \frac{\prod_{0\leq i<j<l}(d_j-d_i)}{\prod_{\mu=0}^{l-1}\mu!}\ne0.
190:     \]
191:     Indeed, $\binom{X}{\mu}$ has degree $\mu$ and leading coefficient
192:     $1/\mu!$, so its evaluation determinant is the Vandermonde determinant
193:     multiplied by $\prod_\mu1/\mu!$. All other contributions have lower total
194:     degree. Thus the new Wronskian, and hence the original one, is nonzero.
195: \end{proof}

206: Suppose $l\geq1$ and $\phi_0(x_1, \dots, x_p), \phi_1(x_1, \dots, x_p), \dots, \phi_{l-1}(x_1, \dots, x_p)$ are $l$ polynomials in the $p$ variables $x_1, \dots, x_p$. Also suppose $\Delta_0, \Delta_1, \dots, \Delta_{l-1}$ are $l$ differential operators such that for each $i$, $0 \leq i \leq l-1$, the order of $\Delta_i$ is at most $i$. We call the determinant
207: $$
208: W(\phi_0, \dots, \phi_{l-1}; \Delta_0, \dots, \Delta_{l-1}) = \det \left(\Delta_\mu \phi_\nu (x_1, \dots, x_p) \right)_{ 0 \leq \mu \leq l-1 \atop 0 \leq \nu \leq l-1}
209: $$
210: a generalized Wronskian. This is understood as a polynomial in the variables $x_1, \dots, x_p$.  If $p > 1$ and $l >1$ we have more than one generalized Wronskian.
211:

213:     If $\phi_0, \dots, \phi_{l-1}$ are polynomials over a characteristic-zero field and are linearly dependent over that field, then all of their generalized Wronskians are identically zero.
214: \end{lem}
