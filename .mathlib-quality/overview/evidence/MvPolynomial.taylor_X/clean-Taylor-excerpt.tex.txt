500: \section{Index}
501: We start with the Taylor expansion of a polynomial in several variables.
502: \begin{thm}[Taylor's expansion for polynomials in several variables]
503:     Let $P(x_1, \dots, x_p)$ be a polynomial with real coefficients in the variables $x_1, \dots, x_p$ of degree at most $r_i$ in $x_i$ for $1 \leq i \leq p$. For each $1 \leq i \leq p$, pick a natural number $d_i \geq r_i$. Let $(\alpha_1, \dots, \alpha_p) \in \R^p$. Then
504:     $$
505:     P(x_1, \dots, x_p) = \sum_{i_1=0}^{d_1} \cdots \sum_{i_p=0}^{d_p} (\Delta_{i_1, \dots, i_p} P)(\alpha_1, \dots, \alpha_p) \prod_{j=1}^p ( x_j - \alpha_j)^{i_j}.
506:     $$
507: \end{thm}
508: \begin{proof}
509:     For a polynomial $P$ let
510:     $$
511:     T_{d_1, \dots, d_p} P(x_1, \dots, x_p) = \sum_{i_1=0}^{d_1} \cdots \sum_{i_p=0}^{d_p} (\Delta_{i_1, \dots, i_p} P)(\alpha_1, \dots, \alpha_p) \prod_{j=1}^p ( x_j - \alpha_j)^{i_j}
512:     $$
513: It is clear that
514: $$
515: T_{d_1, \dots, d_p} (P_1 + P_2) = T_{d_1, \dots, d_p} (P_1) + T_{d_1, \dots, d_p}(P_2)
516: $$
517: and for any constant $c$,
518: $$
519: T_{d_1, \dots, d_p}(c P) = c T_{d_1, \dots, d_p}(P).
520: $$
521: So it suffices to prove the theorem for monomials. For any tuple
522: $0\leq s_i\leq r_i\leq d_i$, distribution gives
523: $$
524: T_{d_1,\dots,d_p}(x_1^{s_1}\cdots x_p^{s_p})
525:  =\prod_{i=1}^pT_{d_i}(x_i^{s_i})=\prod_{i=1}^px_i^{s_i}
526: $$
527: by the one-variable binomial expansion $x_i^{s_i}=((x_i-\alpha_i)+\alpha_i)^{s_i}$.
528: \end{proof}

530: Let $P(x_1, \dots, x_p)$ be a nonzero polynomial with real coefficients in $p$ variables. Let $\alpha_1, \dots, \alpha_p$ be any real numbers. Let $r_1, \dots, r_p$ be any positive numbers. We define the {\it index} $\theta$ of $P$ at the point $(\alpha_1, \dots, \alpha_p)$ relative to $r_1, \dots, r_p$ as follows: We define $\theta$ to be the minimum of all numbers of the form
531: $$
532: \frac{i_1}{r_1} + \cdots + \frac{i_p}{r_p}
533: $$
534: where the minimum is over all tuples $(i_1, \dots, i_p)$ such that
535: $$
536: \Delta_{i_1, \dots, i_p} P(\alpha_1, \dots, \alpha_p) \ne 0.
537: $$
538: When we want to keep track of all data we write
539: $$
540: \ind(P; \alpha_1, \dots, \alpha_p; r_1, \dots, r_p).
541: $$
542: When there is no confusion about the data, we simply write $\ind(P)$.
543: Taylor's theorem shows that at least one derivative at the chosen point is
544: nonzero; only finitely many derivative multi-indices can be nonzero. Thus
545: this is an attained minimum. We additionally set $\ind(0)=+\infty$.
546: All comparisons involving zero polynomials below use this convention.
